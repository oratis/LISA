\title{Compression Gain as an Admission Criterion\\
       for Concepts Learned at Inference Time}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{abstract}
A deployed language model that is taught a new term by its conversation partner has two
options today: forget it when the context closes, or write it to a persistent store
unconditionally. Neither is learning. Learning requires deciding, without an answer key,
whether what the model \emph{took} the term to mean is what the partner \emph{meant}.
We propose an admission gate whose criterion is \textbf{compression gain}, resting on the
prediction--compression duality: a candidate
concept is consolidated only if conditioning on it compresses the partner's subsequent,
held-out usage by more than the concept's own description length, and by more than
surface-matched placebo concepts. Admission gates are not new, and neither is placing one
at inference time; what is new is the criterion. Every ground-truth-free gate we could
find scores a candidate by how consistently the model already endorses it, how novel it
is, or how trustworthy its source is --- never by how much it compresses what the
interlocutor does next.
On a procedurally generated language of \emph{gavagai pairs} --- teaching sets that are
extensionally identical under a true meaning \M{} and a tempting misreading \Mp{}, separated
only by held-out usage --- compression gain separates \M{} from \Mp{} at AUC $0.915$
($23/24$, $p{=}1.5\mathrm{e}{-}6$) on a frozen 1.5B model with no training, while semantic
entropy reaches $0.269$. The comparison is not merely a gap: stratified by whether \Mp{} is
\emph{narrower} than \M{}, semantic entropy scores $0.137$ --- it systematically prefers the
misreading --- because narrowness inflates confidence, and the narrower misreading is the
most common form of referential ambiguity.
Three preconditions, none of which appeared in our original design, govern whether the
signal exists at all, and we report them with the ablation that would have produced a false
positive. We also report what a parametric consolidation substrate does \emph{not} buy: a
word-substitution control shows it stores a persistent, partition-isolated,
\emph{word-independent} bias over objects; forcing genuine word binding costs the
cross-format transfer that made it attractive; and a token-triggered lexical entry, which
binds by construction, recovers binding completely ($0.835 \to 0.080$ under substitution of
the opposite-meaning word) but transfer only partially ($0.775$ against $0.995$ for context
injection). Two controls that each break one design constraint fail exactly as predicted,
so the diagnosis is sound and the gap is real.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Large language models acquire concepts almost exclusively at training time. When a
conversation partner introduces a genuinely new one --- a term of art, a private
convention, a word of a language the model has never seen --- today's deployed systems
either forget it when the context window closes, or write it into some persistent store
\emph{unconditionally}. Neither behaviour is learning. Learning would require the model to
form a hypothesis about what the partner means, test that hypothesis against how the
partner actually uses the term, and only then commit it to permanent, partner-specific
memory.

We study concept acquisition \emph{in referencing}: during inference-time interaction,
after pretraining is over. Doing this properly requires four properties simultaneously.
The concept is \textbf{novel} (outside the training distribution, not retrieved); its
acquisition is \textbf{self-verified without ground truth} (no answer key grades the
concept); it is \textbf{persistent} (survives the end of the session); and it is
\textbf{isolated per interlocutor} (one partner's idiolect never leaks into another's).
This paper focuses on the hardest of the four: \emph{absent any answer key, what signal
should decide whether a newly acquired concept deserves permanent consolidation?}

The criterion itself stands on the duality between prediction and compression
\citep{deletang2024compression}: a model that predicts an interlocutor's next usage well
is, by construction, a good code for it, so ``does this concept help me predict you'' and
``does this concept pay for itself in bits'' are the same question asked twice.

\paragraph{The pipeline is not the bottleneck; the admission policy is.}
Context-distillation methods already internalize in-context knowledge into parameters ---
context distillation onto the model itself \citep{snell2022distilling},
self-distillation onto an adapter with the model as its own teacher
\citep{kujanpaa2024injection}, trained KV-prefixes \citep{eyuboglu2025cartridges},
per-document adapters generated by a hypernetwork \citep{tan2025dyprag},
on-policy reverse-KL variants \citep{ye2026opcd} --- and Generative Adapter
\citep{chen2024generativeadapter} maps a context to an adapter in a single forward pass,
producing parameter deltas that can be stored and reused per user. What these pipelines
share is the admission policy: \emph{whatever enters the context gets written}. Prompt
Distillation applies no screening to its self-generated training data. Cartridges trains
on every generated conversation. OPCD's only selective variant scores candidates by task
performance on a \emph{labeled} validation set; in the setting its authors explicitly
designate as label-free, what gets distilled ``is not pre-evaluated'' and selection is
random.

\paragraph{Gates exist --- the open axis is the criterion.}
Across continual learning, memory architectures and the 2026 agent-memory wave, the
admission signals of persistent-consolidation gates fall into ten proxy families
(Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}; \S\ref{sec:related}). We claim novelty on none of the obvious axes. Consensus gating
has already realized a ground-truth-free correctness proxy: GATES \citep{stein2026gates}
admits a distillation item only when $\geq 4$ of $8$ tutor rollouts agree, and LMSI
\citep{huang2022selfimprove} filtered self-training data by majority vote as early as 2022.
Nor is the \emph{locus} ours: ConsistencyGate \citep{zhang2026consistencygate} places a
ground-truth-free admission gate exactly where ours sits --- at deployment time, per
candidate fact, before a persistent write. What remains open is the criterion type alone:
\textbf{no existing method uses compression gain --- a minimum-description-length criterion
--- as the admission signal.} Compression \emph{has} been proposed as the currency for
agent memory: as an agenda in description-length form \citep{colaco2026ratedistortion},
and, closest to us, as a worked decision-centric rate--distortion theory
\citep{zou2026demem}, which independently argues that memory should preserve
\emph{decisions} rather than \emph{descriptions} --- a conclusion our own ablations reach
empirically (\S\ref{sec:observable}). Three things separate that line from ours. It
compresses \textbf{the agent's own action choices}, whereas our criterion is evaluated on
\textbf{another party's subsequent word choices}. It answers \textbf{what may be forgotten
under a budget}, a partition problem, whereas ours answers \textbf{whether a candidate
should be written at all}. And its distortion is decision regret, so it presumes an
observed reward at each step; ours presumes only that the interlocutor keeps talking.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{A map with a corrected gap.} A ten-family taxonomy of admission signals
across all persistent-consolidation gates we could verify, including the closest positive
precedents --- consensus gates that already realize ground-truth-free correctness proxies,
both at training-time data admission
\citep{stein2026gates,huang2022selfimprove} and, as of July 2026, at exactly our locus
\citep{zhang2026consistencygate}. \emph{The gap we occupy is one axis, not two: the
criterion type.} We further show \emph{why} that distinction matters rather than asserting
it --- the consistency signal the closest precedents rely on is not merely weaker on our
task but systematically inverted on its most common form.

\item \textbf{A mechanism.} A two-part MDL admission gate with a placebo control, measured
on held-out usage decisions, upgraded to a three-state (accept / reject / ask) design by a
ground-truth-free decidability check; per-item calibration against a null distribution and
a dimensionless rank statistic that transfers a single threshold across model scales.

\item \textbf{A benchmark that constructs the failure mode.} Gavagai pairs whose teaching
sets are extensionally consistent with both \M{} and \Mp{}, so that ``confident, consistent
misunderstanding'' is a controlled experimental condition rather than an anecdote --- with
ground truth available to the \emph{evaluator} but never to the gate.

\item \textbf{An honest scope, including a negative result about the substrate.} We report
three preconditions, the ablation that would have produced a false positive, and a
word-substitution control showing that our parametric consolidation stores a persistent,
partition-isolated, \emph{word-independent} bias over objects rather than the meaning of a
word --- together with the trade-off that forcing genuine binding destroys cross-format
transfer.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Where the signals fall.}
Continual and lifelong learning has produced a rich taxonomy of \emph{what to keep}.
Reservoir and class-balanced sampling admit examples by randomness or label counts
\citep{prabhu2020gdumb,chaudhry2019tiny}; gradient-based selection chooses by gradient
diversity \citep{aljundi2019gss}, while maximally-interfered retrieval is a
\emph{retrieval}-side criterion rather than an admission one \citep{aljundi2019mir};
herding stores class-representative exemplars \citep{rebuffi2017icarl}; dark experience
replay stores reservoir-sampled logits \citep{buzzega2020derpp}. Architectural growth gates
decide \emph{when to expand} on training loss, parameter drift, or data likelihood
\citep{yoon2017den,lee2020cndpm}. Self-adapting schemes gate their own edits on a downstream binary reward
\citep{zweiger2025seal}. Modern write-side gates admit items by geometric novelty
\citep{wang2026sage}, normalized-loss hysteresis \citep{li2025selfsizing}, or Bayesian
surprise \citep{gorlo2026worth}. The 2026 agent-memory wave gates writes on trust proxies
--- source reputation and reliability \citep{zahn2026writetime}, or faithfulness to the
source \citep{yang2026trustmem}. None of these signals assesses whether the acquired
concept itself matches what the interlocutor meant.

\paragraph{Uncertainty is the wrong property here.}
Semantic entropy flags \emph{inconsistency} \citep{farquhar2024semantic} --- but a
confidently held misunderstanding is low-entropy: self-consistent, novel to the memory
store, often surprising, faithfully transcribed from a trusted source. Every existing
signal class therefore admits it, while it fails to predict how the teacher will use the
term next. That predictive failure is exactly what compression gain measures, and agreement-based
confidence has itself been audited as an unreliable standalone correctness signal
\citep{ding2026agree}. Naive
self-reflection without external verification does not repair errors
\citep{huang2024cannot}, and self-improvement cannot create information absent from the
model --- a data-processing-inequality argument \citep{cover1999elements} invoked as
motivation by sharpening analyses \citep{huang2024sharpening} --- which is why the new
information in our setting must come from the interlocutor's usage.

\paragraph{Boundary.}
Three neighbouring problems are not ours. Source-trust gates
\citep{zahn2026writetime,yang2026trustmem} address whether the \textbf{teacher} is
reliable; our gate addresses whether the \textbf{model's hypothesis matches what the
teacher means}. Correctness gates that consult an answer key or task outcome
\citep{zelikman2022star} presuppose exactly the signal our setting denies. And post-hoc
compaction \citep{kim2026memrefine,zou2026demem} decides what to \emph{drop} from an
already-written store under a budget, whereas we decide what is \emph{admitted} in the
first place. All three are orthogonal to ours and composable with it; none subsumes it, and
we evaluate only admission.

\section{Method}

\subsection{What the gate observes}
\label{sec:observable}

A concept hypothesis must be scored against \emph{evidence}. The natural-seeming choice ---
the likelihood of the interlocutor's utterances --- fails: the entropy of fluent text is
dominated by syntax and lexical choice, and the few bits that carry conceptual content are
swamped. We therefore score a candidate concept against the interlocutor's \textbf{usage
decisions}: for each held-out instance $o$, whether the interlocutor applies the term
($y_o \in \{\textsf{applies}, \neg\textsf{applies}\}$). Crucially, $y_o$ is
\emph{observed usage} --- data --- and not an answer about whether the concept is right.

This choice is not a detail. Scored on free-form statements the gain is $+0.68$ nats
($5/8$, $p{=}0.36$; the word-level margin is $+0.02 \approx 0$); scored on the decision
sequence it is $+4.92$ nats ($8/8$, $p{=}0.0039$) --- a $7.2\times$ improvement on the same
items and the same model. A parallel argument has been made for agent memory
\citep{zou2026demem}: what deserves to be kept is what preserves \emph{decisions} rather
than \emph{descriptions}. We reach the same conclusion from the opposite direction, by
measuring what happens when the observable is chosen wrongly.

\subsection{A two-part code with a placebo control}

The gain is a two-part (MDL) code: the concept must earn back its own description length.
Writing $K_u$ for the interlocutor's already-consolidated knowledge and $H_u$ for held-out
turns \emph{not} used to form the candidate $c$,
\begin{equation}
G(c) \;=\; \sum_{o \in H_u} \big[-\log P(y_o \mid o, K_u)\big]
        \;-\; \sum_{o \in H_u} \big[-\log P(y_o \mid o, K_u \cup \{c\})\big]
        \;-\; L(c).
\label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
Two controls are load-bearing. \textbf{(i) Held-out turns}: scoring on the teaching turns
themselves is self-compression and inflates the gain. \textbf{(ii) A placebo pool}: since
adding \emph{any} plausible text to the context can lower perplexity, a candidate must beat
the best of a pool of surface-matched but semantically wrong concepts.

We report $\Delta\text{NLL}$ (pure predictive power) and $G$ (with the length penalty)
separately, because their aggregate is misleading. A naive implementation of
Eq.~\ref{eq:gain} appears to succeed at $8/8$, but $92\%$ of that margin comes from $L(c)$
alone; the word-level predictive margin is $\approx 0$. Reporting only $G$ would have
yielded a false positive.

\subsection{Per-item calibration and the abstention state}

Raw gains are not comparable across concepts --- base entropies differ by roughly $2\times$.
We calibrate per item against the placebo pool itself, treating it as a null distribution,
$z_i(c) = (\Delta\text{NLL}_i(c) - \mu_i^{\text{null}}) / \sigma_i^{\text{null}}$. The pool
deliberately contains \emph{over-specific} alternatives ($\M \wedge \text{extra}$), so that
a narrower misreading is a \emph{typical} null member rather than an outlier. The design
prediction holds precisely: \Mp{} averages $-0.22$ against a null centre of $-0.27$ --- a
gap of $0.05$ nats --- while \M{} sits at $+3.86$. Calibration raises AUC from $0.797$ to
$0.906$. The mechanism is inherited from SEMA's novelty calibration
\citep{wang2024sema}; the difference, again, is the signal being calibrated.

A binary gate is not enough. The gate's discriminative power is inherited from the base
model: if the model's predictions do not respond to the candidate at all, any gain-based
criterion is reading noise. We therefore add a \emph{decidability} precondition computed
from the null pool's dispersion $\sigma_{\text{null}}$ --- requiring no knowledge of which
candidate is correct, and already computed by the gate. Below threshold the gate
\textbf{abstains and queries the interlocutor} rather than guessing. This raises AUC from
$0.857$ to $0.964$ with $15/15$ correct acceptances of \M{} and no false rejections;
accuracy on covered items is $90.0\%$. The abstention is justified rather than evasive: on
the abstained items themselves the criterion scores AUC $0.551$, i.e.\ chance --- the gate
declines exactly the items it could not have decided.

For cross-scale deployment we use \texttt{rank}, the within-batch percentile of the raw
score. Being a monotone transform it costs nothing within a batch, and being dimensionless
it transfers: a single threshold $\tau = 0.40$ gives $100\%$ and $92.3\%$ on 1.5B and 3B
respectively. The cost is that percentiles need a batch of candidates --- a same-round pool
or a rolling window.

\subsection{Consolidation and per-interlocutor partitioning}
\label{sec:consolidation}

Concepts that pass the gate are written to a store keyed by
$(\textsf{interlocutor\_id}, \textsf{term})$; retrieval is masked to the querying
interlocutor's partition, so cross-interlocutor interference is zero \emph{by construction}
rather than by ranking. We instantiate this at two levels, and keep them strictly separate
throughout. \textbf{(a) External}: a key--value store whose entries are re-injected into
context; this is what \S\ref{sec:endtoend} uses. \textbf{(b) Parametric}: a sparse
product-key memory whose keys and values are \emph{parameters}, added to the residual
stream at the final layer, with the slot index space partitioned by interlocutor and
gradients masked to the writing partition; the base model is frozen throughout and no
gradient passes through any transformer block. This is what \S\ref{sec:substrate} uses.

One implementation detail is a necessary condition rather than an incidental choice: slot
routing must use \textbf{cosine-normalised} similarity. Prompts differing only in the
described object yield nearly parallel hidden states (pairwise cosine $\approx 0.994$ at
$\lVert h \rVert \approx 172$), so raw dot-product routing saturates the softmax, sends
every input to the same slot, and degenerates the memory into a constant bias.

\section{Experimental setup}

\paragraph{A constructed language, with contamination controls.}
Testing acquisition of \emph{novel} concepts requires materials provably outside the
training distribution. Real low-resource languages cannot give this guarantee at scale
(Unicode, code comments, loanwords, parallel-corpus leakage). We therefore generate a
controlled language procedurally: pseudo-words filtered against word lists and tokenizer
familiarity, over a combinatorial micro-world whose semantics are known by construction.
Two independent contamination checks pass: tokenizer filtering removes $0$ items, and a
zero-shot probe on the pseudo-words gives AUC $0.394$ --- no usable prior.

\paragraph{Gavagai pairs.}
Each item is a \emph{gavagai pair}: a true meaning \M{} and a tempting misreading \Mp{} that
are \textbf{extensionally identical on the teaching set} --- both explain every teaching
example perfectly --- and are separated \textbf{only by held-out usage}. This renders
Quine's indeterminacy as a controlled, scorable condition. Five ambiguity families are
covered: conjunction ($\Mp = \M \wedge \text{extra}$), category level,
material-vs-object, argument order, and absolute-vs-relative properties.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Unless stated otherwise: frozen Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, pure prompting, \textbf{no
training}, single machine. The one exception is the parametric memory of
\S\ref{sec:substrate}, where the base model remains entirely frozen and only the memory's
keys and values are trained. We report AUC (threshold-free), win counts with sign tests,
and item-level bootstrap CIs. We use threshold-free AUC deliberately: the model has a
systematic \textsf{No}-bias on unfamiliar pseudo-word predicates --- under
\texttt{komalor means blue}, blue items score $p_{\textsf{yes}} \in [0.245, 0.349]$ against
red items at $[0.020, 0.023]$, a $\approx 14\times$ discriminative signal that lies
\emph{entirely below} $0.5$. A $0.5$-threshold accuracy would read $0.00$. This also
supports the mechanism: the gate consumes continuous NLL rather than thresholded decisions
and is immune to the bias.

\section{Results}

\subsection{The gate separates a true concept from a self-consistent misreading}

On the core domain --- conjunction, category level and material-vs-object, the families
where the base model demonstrably applies definitions compositionally --- compression gain
separates \M{} from \Mp{} at \textbf{AUC $0.915$} ($23/24$, $p = 1.5\mathrm{e}{-}6$), while
semantic entropy reaches $0.269$. Across all five families the figures are $0.809$
($37/40$, $p = 9.7\mathrm{e}{-}9$) and $0.423$. Procedurally generated items reproduce the
hand-built set: core-domain margin $+4.44$ nats ($23/24$, AUC$(z)$ $0.911$) against
$+4.08$ ($19/22$).

Two methodological facts must accompany these numbers. First, our ``semantic entropy'' and
``epistemic-drop'' arms are \emph{mathematically equivalent} for the paired \M{}-vs-\Mp{}
decision (the base entropy cancels in the difference; measured per-item margin differs by
$5.55\mathrm{e}{-}17$), so there are \textbf{two independent arms, not three}. A genuine
expected-information-gain querying arm is not implemented. Second, we use a
\emph{binary-exact} semantic entropy computed directly from logits --- a stronger variant
than the sampled original --- at the cost of the bidirectional-entailment clustering, so
conclusions could differ in a free-generation setting.

\paragraph{The failure is directional, and that is the point.}
Semantic entropy does not merely fail here; it fails \emph{in the wrong direction}.
Stratifying by whether \Mp{} is narrower than \M{} (Figure~\ref{fig:criterion}): where it
is (conjunction, category level) semantic entropy scores $\mathbf{0.137}$ --- it systematically \emph{prefers} the
misreading; where it is not, it sits at chance ($0.507$). The mechanism is visible in the
entropies themselves: a narrower concept licenses more confident rejections ($H$ falls
$0.363 \to 0.253$ and $0.308 \to 0.148$). Self-consistency measures \emph{how certain the
model is}, and narrowness inflates certainty. On the most common form of referential
ambiguity --- the Quinean one, where the misreading is a proper subset --- that is exactly
the wrong bias. We report the exception: on absolute-vs-relative items, the one family
where \Mp{} is \emph{not} narrower, semantic entropy is the better signal ($0.750$).

\subsection{Scope: where the gate works, and why scaling is not a general fix}

The gate's power is inherited from the base model along two axes, and they respond to scale
in \emph{opposite} directions. Where the failure is \emph{no signal} --- the model's
predictions barely move when given the definition --- scaling helps: argument-order items
go from AUC $0.562$ to $0.812$ between 1.5B and 3B. Where the failure is
\emph{conditioning failure} --- the model's priors override the definition it was given ---
scaling \textbf{hurts}: absolute-vs-relative items fall from $0.812$ to $\mathbf{0.000}$.
Aggregate margins grew $4\times$ while discrimination quality fell ($0.857 \to 0.738$).
\textbf{Larger is not a general remedy.}

Conditioning failure is not a formatting artifact. Before drawing this conclusion we
verified the apparatus on trivial commonsense items, which the 3B model answers $6/6$ in
both raw and chat framings; it nonetheless fails a framing-free single-attribute
application (\texttt{kirel means red} followed by \texttt{Is a red box kirel?} gives
$p(\textsf{yes}) = 0.000$).

\subsection{Isolation is a precondition for correctness, not hygiene}

With eight conflict pairs --- the same pseudo-word taught two incompatible meanings by two
interlocutors --- partitioned retrieval yields perfect discrimination (AUC $1.000$, gate
$8/8$). Sharing the store collapses both: discrimination falls to $0.754$ and the gate
drops to $50\%$, i.e.\ chance, with the worst item inverted at $0.281$. This is not a model
failure but an \emph{information} one: when two mutually exclusive definitions are
simultaneously present, the observed data no longer contains which one belongs to whom.

Three scope notes. The shared-condition gate comparison varies only the \emph{order} of the
same two sentences, so it measures whether position carries attribution (it does not); it
should not be described as the gate choosing wrongly between two candidates. The shared
condition is a \emph{strong} form of contamination, not a naturalistic leak. And the
partitioning here is at the context level.

\subsection{End-to-end: all four properties at once}
\label{sec:endtoend}

Finally we run the full loop with the external store of \S\ref{sec:consolidation}(a):
teach $\to$ gate $\to$ consolidate into the interlocutor's partition $\to$ \textbf{clear
the context} $\to$ new session, loading only that partition. Across 16 interlocutors the
gate selects the true meaning $16/16$ against a candidate set that includes the
\emph{other} interlocutor's meaning; after the context is cleared, probe AUC rises from
$0.514$ (no memory) to $0.980$ (own partition), while loading another interlocutor's
concept gives $0.129$. Cross-partition hits: $0/16$.

This gate task is substantially easier than the gavagai discrimination above --- four
mutually exclusive colours, decided by a $+9.92$ nat margin --- and the two numbers should
not be read as comparable. We also report the shared-store failure mode, which is
\emph{last-write-wins} rather than degradation: the first writer's concept is destroyed
(AUC $0.148$) while the last writer is intact ($0.977$), so the mean of $0.562$ is
misleading and we report the two separately.

\section{What a parametric substrate does and does not buy}
\label{sec:substrate}

An admitted concept has to live somewhere. Replacing the external store with the parametric
memory of \S\ref{sec:consolidation}(b) removes the retrieval step: with \textbf{no mention
of the concept anywhere in the context}, probe AUC rises from $0.485$ (frozen base, at
chance) to $0.992$, and querying another interlocutor's partition gives $0.260$. Sequential
writing shows what the partition is doing. Sixteen interlocutors written one after another
into \emph{partitioned} slots leave earlier ones untouched --- but this is true \emph{by
construction}, since gradients are masked to the writing partition, and we verify it holds
exactly. The informative comparison is the ablation: a \emph{shared} pool of identical total
capacity collapses from the \emph{second} write ($1.000 \to 0.520$) and ends at $0.562$
with several interlocutors at $0.000$. What prevents interference is the \textbf{partition},
not capacity or sparsity.

\paragraph{A word-substitution control undercuts the natural reading of those numbers.}
Replacing the taught pseudo-word with an unrelated one --- leaving every other token intact
--- leaves probe AUC essentially unchanged ($0.945 \to 0.930$ mid-network;
$0.970 \to 0.975$ at the output layer). Teaching the same interlocutor \emph{two} words does
not force binding either: substituting that interlocutor's \emph{other} word moves the first
word's AUC by $-0.005$, although both words are individually learned. The mechanism differs
by insertion point. Mid-network, retrieval is blind to the word: routed-slot overlap after
substitution is $1.000$. At the output layer the memory output \emph{does} vary with the
word (cosine $0.787$), but only along directions orthogonal to the decision --- the
positive-minus-negative projection onto $W_{\textsf{yes}} - W_{\textsf{no}}$ is preserved at
$97\%$ ($+4.443 \to +4.327$), so the ranking, and therefore the AUC, is unchanged.
\textbf{We therefore claim only a persistent, partition-isolated, word-independent bias over
objects} --- not that a word's meaning has been learned.

\paragraph{Binding is achievable, and it costs the transfer.}
The failure above is a property of the teaching set, not of the architecture. Training on a
set where two words assign \emph{opposite} labels to the same object makes any
word-independent policy provably chance-level, and the memory then learns to condition on
the word: substituting the opposite-meaning word flips the probe from $0.925$ to
$\mathbf{0.070}$. But the cross-format transfer that made the mid-network variant attractive
disappears at the same time: on held-out decision formats the bound memory scores $0.545$
and $0.670$ against a base of $0.540$ and $0.525$, where the \emph{unbound} variant reached
$0.935$ and $0.945$. The earlier transfer was a consequence of \emph{not} binding
(Figure~\ref{fig:substrate}): an unbound memory tags the object representation, and any
readout format can read that tag.
Context injection obtains both properties ($0.905$ and $0.995$).

\paragraph{A better substrate narrows the gap without closing it.}
The two failures above suggest what a substrate would have to do: key on something that
identifies the \emph{word} and is observable at inference, and write its value into the
\emph{representation} rather than into the logits. Context injection satisfies both --- the
key is the term's own token, resolved by attention, and the retrieved content enters the
residual stream. We therefore build the parameter-space analogue, a \emph{token-triggered
lexical entry}: one learned vector per (interlocutor, term), added to the residual stream at
a mid layer, at exactly the positions where that term's tokens occur, with no similarity
search of any kind.

Binding then holds by construction, and the measurements confirm it: substituting the
opposite-meaning word drops the probe from $0.835$ to $\mathbf{0.080}$ --- the answer follows
the substituted word rather than merely degrading --- and substituting an unrelated word
returns it to base ($0.550$), because the trigger does not fire at all. Compare the
$0.017$ drop of the unbound memory above. Cross-format transfer improves as well, but only
partially: $0.775$ on the held-out True/False format against a base of $0.525$ and a
previous best of $0.670$. \textbf{Across every configuration we tested, the parametric route
attains at most one of the two \emph{at full strength}}; the best point we found holds
binding completely and transfer partially, against the $0.995$ that context injection obtains
for free.

Three checks make this a clean negative rather than an inconclusive one. Two deliberately
break one constraint each and fail exactly as predicted: writing the same value into the
logits instead of the representation reproduces the base model to three decimals, and
dropping the token trigger --- injecting at every position --- leaves the training objective
\emph{unfittable} ($0.531$ against a $0.500$ floor), since a position-independent constant
cannot tell which of two terms is being asked about. The attribution is therefore right; what
was wrong was our expectation that satisfying both constraints would suffice. Third, a
diagnosis of the A/B format shows its stubbornness is not a resolution artifact --- the
entry's score distribution is \emph{wider} than context injection's, not compressed --- but
genuine non-transfer, and that this format is in any case an unreliable instrument for
non-contextual methods, with per-interlocutor base AUC ranging from $0.20$ to $0.84$; the
True/False format, where the base is flat at $0.525$ and context injection reaches $0.995$,
is the fair test. Finally, adding an explicit identity-preservation term to the objective ---
so that the entry enriches rather than replaces the word's representation --- neither helps
nor is needed: the model can already reproduce the term under injection, and the term costs
binding strength ($0.080 \to 0.135$) without moving transfer.

\section{Limitations}

\textbf{(i) No substrate we built obtains both binding and cross-format transfer.} An
unbound memory stores a word-independent bias over objects; forcing binding costs the
transfer; and a token-triggered lexical entry --- which keys on the word by construction ---
recovers binding fully but transfer only partially ($0.775$ against $0.995$ for context
injection). We tried the obvious repair (an identity-preservation term) and it does not help.
What we have \emph{not} tried is relaxing the entry from a constant offset to a small
input-dependent transformation, which is where we would look next.
The two arms of the binding experiment are also not matched on optimisation --- the
mid-network memory reaches zero training loss by step 150 and is trained to 900 --- and
per-interlocutor variance in held-out generalisation is large. We forced binding only in the
mutually-exclusive two-word form, where the memory need encode one axis plus a
word-dependent sign.

\textbf{(ii) The gate verifies usage fidelity, not world truth.} It rejects the model's
\emph{misreadings} of what the interlocutor means; a consistently deceptive interlocutor
will be believed. This is a designed boundary, composable with provenance-based gates that
address the orthogonal question of whether the source is trustworthy.

\textbf{(iii) Conditioning failure is unsolved.} Where the base model's priors override the
supplied definition, gain-based criteria read a mixture of prior and concept, and the effect
\emph{worsens} with scale. The decidability meter detects only undecidability, not confident
error; the three-state gate still admits three \Mp{} items.

\textbf{(iv) Two arms, not three.} A genuine expected-information-gain querying arm is not
implemented, so the abstention state's ``ask'' branch is evaluated only as abstention.

\textbf{(iv-b) Two engineering hazards of any reference-then-internalize loop remain
untested here.} Re-supplying the original context to a model that has already internalized
it can \emph{degrade} performance \citep{wang2026whencontext}, and iterated
experience-internalization has been reported to collapse progressively rather than compound
\citep{chen2026continual}. Our gate is a plausible mitigation for the second --- it is
precisely a filter on what gets written --- but we have not tested that, and the cited work
does not attribute the collapse to indiscriminate writing; we flag it as a hypothesis, not
a result.

\textbf{(v) Single model family, small item counts, item-level (not seed-level) CIs}, and
usage decisions are given by construction rather than extracted from natural dialogue. We
also do not evaluate composition under negation, quantification, or multi-hop chains.

% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}

Deciding whether a newly acquired concept deserves permanent memory is an admission
problem, and the field has converged on gates whose signal is consistency, novelty, or
source trust. We argued for a different currency --- how much the candidate compresses the
interlocutor's subsequent, held-out usage --- and showed on a controlled benchmark of
gavagai pairs that it separates a true meaning from a self-consistent misreading where the
consistency signal is systematically inverted. The result comes with three preconditions
that were not in our original design, an ablation that would otherwise have produced a false
positive, and a negative result about where an admitted concept should be stored. We think
the last of these is the more useful contribution to the substrate question: a parametric
store can be made to bind to a word, or to transfer across decision formats, but on our
evidence not both.

\section{What we retracted}
\label{app:retractions}

Several claims in earlier drafts of this work did not survive our own checks, and the
corrections changed the contribution statement rather than merely its wording. We list the
ones a reader of the current text might otherwise reconstruct incorrectly.
\emph{(a)} We initially claimed no ground-truth-free write-admission gate existed; GATES and
LMSI refute this, and we now claim nothing on that axis.
\emph{(b)} We then claimed the \emph{locus} --- inference-time, per-item, persistent writing
--- as a second axis of novelty; ConsistencyGate refutes this, and the claim is now confined
to the criterion.
\emph{(c)} We reported that our parametric memory had internalized a concept; a
word-substitution control shows it had not (\S6), and every statement about that substrate
is now scoped to a word-independent bias over objects.
\emph{(d)} We attributed the ``self-improvement cannot create information'' argument to a
sharpening analysis; it is a data-processing-inequality argument and is cited as such.
